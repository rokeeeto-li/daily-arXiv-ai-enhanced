### [1] [Benchmarking Classical Coverage Path Planning Heuristics on Irregular Hexagonal Grids for Maritime Coverage Scenarios](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15202)
*Carlos S. Sepúlveda,Gonzalo A. Ruz*

Main category: cs.RO

TL;DR: A reproducible benchmark of 10,000 irregular hexagonal grid instances and 17 heuristics for coverage path planning, revealing that residual degree definition (not tie-breaking) dominates performance and that Hamiltonian heuristics rarely achieve zero-revisit tours despite solving the relaxed task reliably.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Coverage path planning on irregular hexagonal grids is critical for maritime surveillance, search and rescue, and environmental monitoring, yet classical methods are typically evaluated on small ad hoc examples or rectangular grids rather than realistic maritime scenarios.

Method: A benchmark on synthetic maritime-motivated irregular hexagonal graphs containing 10,000 Hamiltonian-feasible instances across compact, elongated, and irregular morphologies. The evaluation includes 17 heuristics from seven families and a common protocol measuring Hamiltonian success, complete-coverage success, revisits, path length, heading changes, and CPU latency.

Result: Heuristics with explicit shortest-path reconnection solve the relaxed coverage task reliably but almost never produce zero-revisit tours. The strongest Hamiltonian baseline (79.0% success) is a Warnsdorff variant using index-based tie-breaking with terminal-inclusive residual-degree policy. Exact DFS confirms all instances are Hamiltonian-feasible.

Conclusion: The benchmark serves as a controlled testbed for heuristic analysis rather than a claim of operational optimality at fleet scale. Implementation details—particularly residual degree definition when reserving the endpoint—materially affect performance on sparse geometric graphs with bottlenecks, demonstrating that underreported design choices can significantly impact results.

Abstract: Coverage path planning on irregular hexagonal grids is relevant to maritime surveillance, search and rescue and environmental monitoring, yet classical methods are often compared on small ad hoc examples or on rectangular grids. This paper presents a reproducible benchmark of deterministic single-vehicle coverage path planning heuristics on irregular hexagonal graphs derived from synthetic but maritime-motivated areas of interest. The benchmark contains 10,000 Hamiltonian-feasible instances spanning compact, elongated, and irregular morphologies, 17 heuristics from seven families, and a common evaluation protocol covering Hamiltonian success, complete-coverage success, revisits, path length, heading changes, and CPU latency. Across the released dataset, heuristics with explicit shortest-path reconnection solve the relaxed coverage task reliably but almost never produce zero-revisit tours. Exact Depth-First Search confirms that every released instance is Hamiltonian-feasible. The strongest classical Hamiltonian baseline is a Warnsdorff variant that uses an index-based tie-break together with a terminal-inclusive residual-degree policy, reaching 79.0% Hamiltonian success. The dominant design choice is not tie-breaking alone, but how the residual degree is defined when the endpoint is reserved until the final move. This shows that underreported implementation details can materially affect performance on sparse geometric graphs with bottlenecks. The benchmark is intended as a controlled testbed for heuristic analysis rather than as a claim of operational optimality at fleet scale.

### [2] [A Hierarchical Spatiotemporal Action Tokenizer for In-Context Imitation Learning in Robotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15215)
*Fawad Javed Fateh,Ali Shah Ali,Murad Popattia,Usman Nizamani,Andrey Konin,M. Zeeshan Zia,Quoc-Huy Tran*

Main category: cs.RO

TL;DR: The paper introduces HiST-AT, a hierarchical spatiotemporal action tokenizer for in-context imitation learning, using multi-level clustering to reconstruct actions and recover timestamps, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multiple robotic manipulation benchmarks.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing action tokenizers for in-context imitation learning are limited to non-hierarchical approaches that only exploit spatial information. The authors aim to develop a more effective representation by incorporating both spatial and temporal information through a hierarchical structure.

Method: The method consists of two contributions: 1) A hierarchical approach with two levels of vector quantization (lower level assigns actions to fine-grained subclusters, higher level maps subclusters to clusters); 2) An extended hierarchical spatiotemporal action tokenizer (HiST-AT) that conducts multi-level clustering while simultaneously recovering input actions and their timestamps.

Result: Extensive evaluations on multiple simulation and real robotic manipulation benchmarks demonstrate that HiST-AT establishes new state-of-the-art performance in in-context imitation learning, outperforming both non-hierarchical counterparts and other approaches.

Conclusion: The hierarchical spatiotemporal approach effectively leverages both spatial and temporal information through multi-level clustering, providing superior action representation for in-context imitation learning tasks compared to non-hierarchical methods.

Abstract: We present a novel hierarchical spatiotemporal action tokenizer for in-context imitation learning. We first propose a hierarchical approach, which consists of two successive levels of vector quantization. In particular, the lower level assigns input actions to fine-grained subclusters, while the higher level further maps fine-grained subclusters to clusters. Our hierarchical approach outperforms the non-hierarchical counterpart, while mainly exploiting spatial information by reconstructing input actions. Furthermore, we extend our approach by utilizing both spatial and temporal cues, forming a hierarchical spatiotemporal action tokenizer, namely HiST-AT. Specifically, our hierarchical spatiotemporal approach conducts multi-level clustering, while simultaneously recovering input actions and their associated timestamps. Finally, extensive evaluations on multiple simulation and real robotic manipulation benchmarks show that our approach establishes a new state-of-the-art performance in in-context imitation learning.

### [3] [Vision-Based Safe Human-Robot Collaboration with Uncertainty Guarantees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15221)
*Jakob Thumm,Marian Frei,Tianle Ni,Matthias Althoff,Marco Pavone*

Main category: cs.RO

TL;DR: A framework combining aleatoric uncertainty estimation with OOD detection to provide conformal prediction guarantees for certifiably safe vision-based human pose estimation and motion prediction in human-robot collaboration settings.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Human-robot collaboration requires certifiable safety guarantees, especially for vision-based pose estimation and motion prediction. Traditional approaches lack rigorous statistical guarantees, making it difficult to ensure safe interaction in real-world scenarios.

Method: Combines aleatoric uncertainty estimation with out-of-distribution (OOD) detection for high probabilistic confidence. Introduces conformal prediction sets for human motion predictions to integrate the pipeline into certifiable safety frameworks, providing statistically valid confidence intervals.

Result: Evaluated on recorded human motion data and a real-world human-robot collaboration setting, demonstrating the framework's effectiveness in providing high, valid confidence guarantees for safe human-robot interaction.

Conclusion: The proposed approach successfully integrates conformal prediction guarantees into vision-based human pose estimation and motion prediction, enabling certifiably safe human-robot collaboration by providing statistically valid confidence measures that can be incorporated into safety-critical systems.

Abstract: We propose a framework for vision-based human pose estimation and motion prediction that gives conformal prediction guarantees for certifiably safe human-robot collaboration. Our framework combines aleatoric uncertainty estimation with OOD detection for high probabilistic confidence. To integrate our pipeline in certifiable safety frameworks, we propose conformal prediction sets for human motion predictions with high, valid confidence. We evaluate our pipeline on recorded human motion data and a real-world human-robot collaboration setting.

### [4] [Abstract Sim2Real through Approximate Information States](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15289)
*Yunfu Deng,Yuhao Li,Josiah P. Hanna*

Main category: cs.RO

TL;DR: This paper formalizes and studies the sim2real problem with abstract simulators that lack key task details. It introduces a method using real-world data to correct abstract simulator dynamics, enabling successful policy transfer to the real world.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Reinforcement learning in robotics relies on fast, accurate simulators, but simulators become harder to obtain as robots are deployed in increasingly complex domains. Simulators often fail to model all relevant task details, motivating the study of sim2real transfer with abstract simulators that omit key task components.

Method: The authors formalize the problem using state abstraction from RL literature, showing that abstract simulators can be grounded to match target tasks when grounded abstract dynamics account for state history. They introduce a method that uses real-world task data to correct the dynamics of the abstract simulator.

Result: The proposed method enables successful policy transfer in both sim2sim and sim2real evaluations, demonstrating that correcting abstract simulator dynamics with real-world data is effective for sim2real transfer.

Conclusion: By formally characterizing sim2real transfer with abstract simulators and developing a data-driven method to correct their dynamics, the authors provide a framework for training policies in abstract simulators and successfully deploying them in the real world, even when simulators lack detailed task information.

Abstract: In recent years, reinforcement learning (RL) has shown remarkable success in robotics when a fast and accurate simulator is available for a given task. When using RL and simulation, more simulator realism is generally beneficial but becomes harder to obtain as robots are deployed in increasingly complex and widescale domains. In such settings, simulators will likely fail to model all relevant details of a given target task and this observation motivates the study of sim2real with simulators that leave out key task details. In this paper, we formalize and study the abstract sim2real problem: given an abstract simulator that models a target task at a coarse level of abstraction, how can we train a policy with RL in the abstract simulator and successfully transfer it to the real-world? Our first contribution is to formalize this problem using the language of state abstraction from the RL literature. This framing shows that an abstract simulator can be grounded to match the target task if the grounded abstract dynamics take the history of states into account. Based on the formalism, we then introduce a method that uses real-world task data to correct the dynamics of the abstract simulator. We then show that this method enables successful policy transfer both in sim2sim and sim2real evaluation.
