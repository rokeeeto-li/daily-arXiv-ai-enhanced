### [1] [A virtual-variable-length method for robust inverse kinematics of multi-segment continuum robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02256)
*Weiting Feng,Federico Renda,Yunjie Yang,Francesco Giorgio-Serchi*

Main category: cs.RO

TL;DR: This paper introduces a Virtual-Variable-Length (VVL) method for solving inverse kinematics in multi-segment continuum manipulators by introducing fictitious segment length variations during iteration, achieving higher convergence success rates and faster convergence compared to conventional Jacobian-based solvers across 1.8M+ randomized trials.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Conventional Jacobian-based inverse kinematics solvers for continuum manipulators often exhibit slow convergence and may fail to converge (deadlock), particularly when initialized from neutral/rest configurations, limiting their reliability and performance.

Method: The Virtual-Variable-Length (VVL) method introduces fictitious variations of segment lengths during the IK solution iteration, creating virtual axial degrees of freedom that alleviate constraints and improve convergence behavior, enabling or accelerating convergence compared to traditional approaches.

Result: Across 1.8M+ randomized trials with manipulators of 2-7 segments, VVL achieved up to 20% higher convergence success rate and 40-80% reduction in average iteration count compared to benchmark Jacobian-based and Damped Least Square solvers at accuracy thresholds of 10⁻⁴-10⁻⁸.

Conclusion: Boundary-proximal configurations frequently cause convergence failures in conventional solvers, and the VVL method effectively mitigates such occurrences, demonstrating robust performance across a wide range of test cases, though deadlocks can still occur at arbitrary poses beyond workspace boundaries.

Abstract: This paper proposes a new, robust method to solve the inverse kinematics (IK) of multi-segment continuum manipulators. Conventional Jacobian-based solvers, especially when initialized from neutral/rest configurations, often exhibit slow convergence and, in certain conditions, may fail to converge (deadlock). The Virtual-Variable-Length (VVL) method proposed here introduces fictitious variations of segments' length during the solution iteration, conferring virtual axial degrees of freedom that alleviate adverse behaviors and constraints, thus enabling or accelerating convergence. Comprehensive numerical experiments were conducted to compare the VVL method against benchmark Jacobian-based and Damped Least Square IK solvers. Across more than $1.8\times 10^6$ randomized trials covering manipulators with two to seven segments, the proposed approach achieved up to a 20$\%$ increase in convergence success rate over the benchmark and a 40-80$\%$ reduction in average iteration count under equivalent accuracy thresholds ($10^{-4}-10^{-8}$). While deadlocks are not restricted to workspace boundaries and may occur at arbitrary poses, our empirical study identifies boundary-proximal configurations as a frequent cause of failed convergence and the VVL method mitigates such occurrences over a statistical sample of test cases.

### [2] [Deep Neural Network Based Roadwork Detection for Autonomous Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02282)
*Sebastian Wullrich,Nicolai Steinke,Daniel Goehring*

Main category: cs.RO

TL;DR: A real-time system that detects and localizes roadworks by combining a YOLO neural network with LiDAR data, achieving sub-0.5m localization accuracy on real-world construction sites.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Road construction sites present major challenges for both autonomous vehicles and human drivers due to their highly dynamic and heterogeneous nature.

Method: Combines YOLO neural network with LiDAR data to identify individual roadwork objects, merge them into coherent construction sites, and record outlines in world coordinates. Training used an adapted US dataset and a new dataset collected from test drives in Berlin, Germany.

Result: Real-world evaluations achieved localization accuracy below 0.5 meters on road construction sites.

Conclusion: The system can support traffic authorities with up-to-date roadwork data and could enable autonomous vehicles to navigate construction sites more safely in the future.

Abstract: Road construction sites create major challenges for both autonomous vehicles and human drivers due to their highly dynamic and heterogeneous nature. This paper presents a real-time system that detects and localizes roadworks by combining a YOLO neural network with LiDAR data. The system identifies individual roadwork objects while driving, merges them into coherent construction sites and records their outlines in world coordinates. The model training was based on an adapted US dataset and a new dataset collected from test drives with a prototype vehicle in Berlin, Germany. Evaluations on real-world road construction sites showed a localization accuracy below 0.5 m. The system can support traffic authorities with up-to-date roadwork data and could enable autonomous vehicles to navigate construction sites more safely in the future.

### [3] [Stop Wandering: Efficient Vision-Language Navigation via Metacognitive Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02318)
*Xueying Li,Feng Lyu,Hao Wu,Mingliu Liu,Jia-Nan Liu,Guozi Liu*

Main category: cs.RO

TL;DR: MetaNav is a training-free VLN agent with metacognitive capabilities that integrates spatial memory, history-aware planning, and reflective correction to improve navigation efficiency and reduce VLM queries.


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: Existing VLN agents rely on greedy frontier selection and passive spatial memory, leading to inefficiencies like local oscillation and redundant revisiting. This stems from a lack of metacognitive capabilities - agents cannot monitor exploration progress, diagnose strategy failures, or adapt accordingly.

Method: MetaNav integrates three key components: (1) Spatial memory for building persistent 3D semantic maps, (2) History-aware planning that penalizes revisiting to improve efficiency, and (3) Reflective correction that detects stagnation and uses an LLM to generate corrective rules for guiding future frontier selection.

Result: MetaNav achieves state-of-the-art performance on GOAT-Bench, HM3D-OVON, and A-EQA benchmarks while reducing VLM queries by 20.7%.

Conclusion: Metacognitive reasoning significantly improves the robustness and efficiency of VLN agents by enabling self-monitoring, failure diagnosis, and adaptive strategy adjustment during exploration.

Abstract: Training-free Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) agents powered by foundation models can follow instructions and explore 3D environments. However, existing approaches rely on greedy frontier selection and passive spatial memory, leading to inefficient behaviors such as local oscillation and redundant revisiting. We argue that this stems from a lack of metacognitive capabilities: the agent cannot monitor its exploration progress, diagnose strategy failures, or adapt accordingly. To address this, we propose MetaNav, a metacognitive navigation agent integrating spatial memory, history-aware planning, and reflective correction. Spatial memory builds a persistent 3D semantic map. History-aware planning penalizes revisiting to improve efficiency. Reflective correction detects stagnation and uses an LLM to generate corrective rules that guide future frontier selection. Experiments on GOAT-Bench, HM3D-OVON, and A-EQA show that MetaNav achieves state-of-the-art performance while reducing VLM queries by 20.7%, demonstrating that metacognitive reasoning significantly improves robustness and efficiency.
